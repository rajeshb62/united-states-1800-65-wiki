Overview

This is Volume II of Charles Francis Adams’s multi-volume Memoirs of John Quincy Adams, Comprising Portions of His Diary from 1795 to 1848, not the Library of America Diaries Vol. II. The title page of the extract reads Memoirs of John Quincy Adams… Edited by Charles Francis Adams. Vol. II. Philadelphia, J. B. Lippincott & Co., 1874. The series prints selected diary text with editorial chapter headings; volume numbering continues the chapter sequence across books.

Volume II contains three chapters only:

  • Chapter VII — The Mission to Russia (opens with the 1809 voyage and St. Petersburg residence; the bulk of the volume)
  • Chapter VIII — The Mediation (Russian offer to mediate the Anglo-American war; 1813–1814)
  • Chapter IX — The Negotiation for Peace (departure toward the Ghent commission in spring 1814)

Running page headers preserve month and year (e.g. [August, / 1809.]). OCR is Hathi-style page files (----- PAGE N -----); soft hyphenation and occasional garbled words appear. Front matter includes a brief table of contents (Ch. VII p. 3; Ch. VIII p. 498; Ch. IX p. 603).

This CFA Memoirs volume overlaps in years with parts of LOA Diaries Vol. 1 Chapters IV–V (St. Petersburg; Ghent) but is a different editorial tradition: nineteenth-century family selection and chaptering rather than the 2017 LOA abridgment.

Saturday, August 5th, 1809. PREFIXED to the fourth volume of the manuscript is this invocation : Lord of creation! thou from whom proceed Each honest thought and honorable deed; Parent of life! without whose quickening ray The soul’s deep darkness, knows not how to pray; Oh! let thy mercy teach my lips their task, Or freely grant the boon they ought to ask! Let not yon glorious orb’s returning light Once from these eyes dispel the shades of night, But from my heart spontaneous may arise A prayer sincere and fervent to the skies, That all earth’s choicest favors may attend, And all thy joys, upon my bosom’s friend, That thou wouldst bless with ever-bounteous hand My parents, children, friends, and native land; Nor be my vows to these alone confined: Forgive my foes, and bless all human kind; And whatsoe’er thy wisdom shall decree My future portion on this earth to be, Let thy good Spirit ever nerve my will To thee, and man, my duties to fulfil. I am very unwell, and have strong symptoms of the jaundice; a lassitude which has almost, but not yet quite, suspended all my industry; a listlessness which, without extinguishing the love of life, affects the mind with the sentiment that life is nothing worth; an oppression at the heart, which, without being positive pain, is more distressing than pain itself.
